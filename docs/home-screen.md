# The home screen

Home is a grid of widgets that you arrange. On a phone, long press to arrange; on desktop, drag a widget by its handle and pull its corners to resize. Only the widget you are dragging moves. Widgets have coordinates, not a place in a queue, so the grid can have gaps and can overflow downward. Each widget has its own settings, and the Home tab in Settings has the rest.

## The widgets

- **The sky clock.** A 24-hour dial of the day where you are. See below.
- **New.** Everything unread across chats, mail and invites, newest first, with no AI involved. It replaced the daily digest, which is gone.
- **Chat.** The conversations you were in most recently.
- **Mail.** The newest threads in the inbox.
- **Today.** The day's events and the tasks due within its window, from the ship's calendar. The window is yours to set.
- **Statuses.** What the people you pinned are up to, then everyone else newest first.
- **The assistant.** One tap to ask or to act.

## The sky clock

The dial shows the sun and a correctly phased moon on their own tracks, stars in the night half, cloud banks kept to the daylight arc, rain when it rains, and the day's high and low, tinted by temperature. The sky colour slides from sunrise through deep night, and the horizon dips with your elevation. A moon that is not up is not drawn. The sun and moon snap across midnight rather than sweeping a lap backwards.

Location is GPS, or a place you type, geocoded through Open-Meteo. The weather is Open-Meteo's too. There is no account and no key, and the only request that leaves the device is the forecast for your location.

On Android the same dial is a home-screen widget, painted to a bitmap and ticking on the minute. It reads the day's range, the stars and the cloud from the app's own sky maths, so the two never disagree.

## Navigation around it

On a phone the sections live in a drawer behind the hamburger on the left, with the ship picker on the right. Which items the drawer shows, and in what order, is yours to choose. Settings is pinned, because on a phone it is the only way back to the screen that unhides things. On desktop the sections are a rail, and the thread pane can be resized like the chat list.
